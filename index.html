About

I am an independent researcher working at the intersection of organisation studies and the political economy of technology. My research examines public funding, technological capability and the relationships between public organisations, firms and technology providers. My work includes studies of national AI programmes in the UAE and public software governance in France and Germany.

My experience in software entrepreneurship, university digitalisation and teaching informs my research on how organisations develop, use and maintain digital systems.

Research

My research focuses on how organisations develop and maintain technological capability. I examine how funding arrangements, organisational responsibilities and relationships with technology providers shape who can develop a system, set its priorities and sustain its operation.

Research interests

  • Organisations and technological change
  • Public funding and innovation
  • Technology partnerships and dependence
  • Digital governance and political economy

Current project

Public Support for AI: What Happens After Funding Ends?

I am developing a project on what smaller firms can continue to develop after temporary public support for AI ends. The project examines how funding conditions and relationships with technology providers shape firms’ options for further development and continued use.

Research approach

My current papers use comparative document analysis, source assessment and the reconstruction of dated events. I work with technical documentation, organisational records and public policy materials, distinguishing documented actions from reported outcomes and from what the available evidence cannot establish.
